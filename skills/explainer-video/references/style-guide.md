# Explainer narrative & visual style guide (TED-Ed formula)

Distilled from scene-by-scene analysis of TED-Ed explainers. Use it when the user
brings a topic but no script. One scene = one narration sentence = one visual
metaphor = 8–12 seconds. Target 26–30 scenes ≈ 5:00–5:30, or scale down to
12–14 scenes ≈ 2:30 for a short.

## Story spine (six acts)

1. **Hook (~5 scenes).** Open on an epigraph quote card, then cold-open a real
   person mid-crisis in a strange, specific moment tied to the topic taken to its
   extreme. End the act on a cliffhanger — do NOT resolve it.
2. **Bridge (1–2 scenes).** "Few of us will ever {extreme version} — but research
   shows even {moderate version} can {core benefit}." Introduce the everyperson
   character in the 'before' state. Pose the driving question.
3. **Mechanism core (~8 scenes).** One claim per scene, escalating timescale:
   immediately → within weeks → over years. Every abstract noun gets a concrete
   embodied metaphor (stress = heavy bag dropped; memory = fortress of books).
   End with a punchline reframe: "{action} is {surprising category}."
4. **Everyday application (~5 scenes).** Debunk the famous benchmark number; show
   effortless accumulation in daily vignettes; the most vibrant, saturated frame
   of the whole video shows the idealized environment; one named study with a
   surprising social finding.
5. **Obstacle & fixes (~6 scenes).** "So why doesn't everyone just {action}?" —
   palette turns grey. Show the systemic barrier, who profits from it, one stark
   statistic. Then two fixes: one big named real-world case study with a hard
   number, one small replicable personal measure — color blooms back in.
6. **Bookend + CTA (~3 scenes).** Resolve the hook's cliffhanger in golden-hour
   palette; state the hero's final achievement number; pivot: "but you don't have
   to {extreme version}." Curtain call: every character crosses the frame. End card.

## Narration rules

- Second person throughout ("your brain"), except the bookend story (third person, past).
- Hedge honestly: "is thought to", "one study found", "research suggests".
- Exactly one big number in the hook, one per case study, one in the resolution.
- Each line under ~35 words — it must be speakable in under 14 seconds.

## Visual rules

- Default style string:
  `minimalist 2D line-art cartoon, thin ink outlines, flat muted pastel fills, soft shadows, high-key lighting, clean white background, educational animation style`
- Palette carries emotion: warm golden = triumph; vibrant pastel = the promised
  world; desaturated grey + harsh contrast = the problem state.
- Exactly two recurring characters: the Epic Figure (real-story bookends) and the
  Everyperson (the viewer's stand-in, visibly transformed by the end).
- Camera language even in flat 2D: wides for context, close-ups for emotion,
  lateral tracking during movement, speed ramps on time jumps.
- Metaphors over diagrams, always. Personify organs/objects for one scene of comic
  relief near the middle.
